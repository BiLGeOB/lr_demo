When we report 100 to a court, is it still 100?

In postmortem toxicology, evidential weight is reported as a likelihood ratio. The validity of that number rests on two silent assumptions — and some of the results depend on a third thing: the shape of the reference distributions.

Result: as the variance ratio moves from 0.6 to 1.8, order information stays put — P(LR>1 | H₀) between 55% and 58%, discrimination between 0.80 and 0.90. Weight information swings: calibration loss from 0.14 to 0.95, strong misleading evidence from 0% to 15%, changing sides on the way. This is a mechanism observation, not a claim that forensic LRs favour one party. But it says which figures are reportable: the ones on the left.
